From our doorstep to a continent in crisis

The crises driving our mission

Tumaini Africa began in 2014 as a local commitment to our own community. Sudan and Eastern DRC are, by the numbers, two of the worst humanitarian emergencies on Earth right now — and the instability spreading around them, through the Sahel and the Horn of Africa, is pulling in millions more people. That scale is why we made these crises our main focus. Here is what is actually happening, sourced from the UN and the humanitarian organisations working inside these crises, and where Tumaini Africa honestly stands today.

Figures compiled from OCHA, UNHCR, WHO, WFP, Human Rights Watch, and the International Crisis Group — current as of August 2026.

Where Tumaini Africa stands today

We want to be exact about this: Tumaini Africa's direct programmes are currently local, serving the communities we already know. We do not yet have staff or partners inside Sudan or Eastern DRC. What follows is the honest case for why that needs to change — the real scale of need, and our actual plan to grow toward it, funded by supporters like you.

Sudan

World's largest displacement crisis

Civil war between the Sudanese Armed Forces and the Rapid Support Forces, ongoing since April 2023

~13.6Mpeople displaced — the largest displacement crisis in the world
30M+people in need of humanitarian and protection assistance
4.4M+have fled across borders into neighbouring countries

Sources: WHO, OCHA Sudan Humanitarian Needs and Response Plan 2026, UNHCR

Sudan is now three years into a war that the World Health Organization has called the world's worst combined health and humanitarian crisis. Over twenty areas of the country are in or at risk of famine. Hospitals, water systems, and schools have been destroyed or shut down by fighting, and health workers themselves have become targets — over 170 have been killed since the war began. Landmines and unexploded ordnance now contaminate farmland, homes, and roads, meaning the danger doesn't end when the fighting moves on. Frontlines have shifted through 2025 and 2026: some displaced families have been able to return to Khartoum, while fighting has intensified sharply in Darfur and Kordofan, including a feared siege of El Obeid affecting hundreds of thousands of civilians.

Eastern DRC

Decades-deep conflict, active fighting

M23 rebel advance and over 100 armed groups active in North Kivu, South Kivu, and Ituri

7M+internally displaced across the DRC, over 5 million in the east alone
21M+people in need of humanitarian assistance nationwide
100+armed groups operating in the mineral-rich eastern provinces

Sources: IOM DRC Crisis Response Plan 2026, Global Centre for the Responsibility to Protect, Human Rights Watch

Eastern DRC's conflict traces back to the aftermath of the 1994 Rwandan genocide, and it has never fully ended. The M23 rebel group's resurgence — widely reported by UN investigators to be backed by Rwanda, which Rwanda disputes — captured the major cities of Goma and Bukavu in early 2025, upending years of humanitarian planning almost overnight. Sexual violence and grave violations against children have risen sharply in the conflict zones. A ceasefire and a US-brokered peace agreement have been signed, yet fighting has continued into 2026, most recently spreading into the highlands of South Kivu, with aid groups reporting civilians blocked from fleeing and humanitarian convoys obstructed. An Ebola outbreak in the same region is compounding an already dire situation, with insecurity actively hampering containment efforts.

The Sahel & the Horn of Africa

Spreading instability

Burkina Faso, Mali, Niger, Chad, and the rising risk of an Ethiopia–Eritrea war

24M+people across the Sahel needing humanitarian aid in 2026
5.8Minternally displaced across the Sahel region
2.28Mdisplaced people now hosted by Chad alone, many fleeing Sudan's war

Sources: OCHA Sahel Dashboard 2026, UNHCR Operational Data Portal, International Crisis Group

Violence that began in Mali, Burkina Faso, and Niger is no longer contained to those borders — it is now spilling into coastal West African states, while Chad absorbs both Sahel-region displacement and refugees fleeing Sudan's civil war from the east. Armed groups have shut down over 14,800 schools across the region. Meanwhile, in the Horn of Africa, the International Crisis Group has warned of a genuine risk that rising Ethiopia–Eritrea tensions could ignite a new state-on-state war, one analysts fear could fuse with Sudan's conflict into a single, wider regional crisis. Taken together, this is not a set of isolated emergencies — it is one continuously widening band of instability stretching across the continent.

Our honest plan

How Tumaini Africa is responding

We started this organisation to help the people directly around us — and we're not going to pretend that automatically extends to Sudan or Eastern DRC. It doesn't, not yet. What it does mean is that every gift raised through global awareness of these crises goes toward closing that gap honestly, one funded step at a time.

NowDirect programmes running in the communities we already serve, with 100% of that work locally delivered and publicly reported.
NextActively seeking a vetted implementing partner already operating in the Sahel or Chad's border regions, where Sudanese and regional displacement is heaviest — the fastest honest path to real impact.
Longer termDirect Tumaini Africa presence in the hardest-hit regions, funded and staffed once we have the resources to do it properly and safely.
